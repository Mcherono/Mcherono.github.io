01 — About

Where actuarial rigour meets data science

My background is a bit unusual. I spent four years studying actuarial science, which means I learned to think carefully about uncertainty, risk, and what numbers actually mean before I ever touched a line of Python. Then I went on to do an MSc in Data Science at AIMS Rwanda, graduating with a Distinction.

That combination matters. Most analysts can run a model. Fewer can tell you whether the model's output is actually reliable, or what the business should do differently based on what it found. I can do both.

I work across the full analytics stack, pulling and cleaning data with Python or SQL, finding patterns, building dashboards and pipelines, and communicating findings to people who didn't ask for a statistics lecture. I've worked in insurance, wholesale, and development sector settings, which means I'm used to data that's inconsistent, incomplete, and politically sensitive, and I've increasingly focused on how clean data and simple tools can support programme and stakeholder decisions, not just commercial ones.
